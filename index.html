Sarah Kane

Astrophysicist, Marshall Scholar, and disability advocate.

I am a PhD student in Astronomy at the University of Cambridge and Trinity College. My work sits at the intersection of Galactic archaeology, data-driven spectroscopy, and the study of chemically peculiar stars as tracers of the Milky Way's formation history.

Beyond research, I care deeply about making science more accessible through mentorship, public engagement, and disability and albinism advocacy.

Research

My research uses stellar chemistry and spectroscopy to reconstruct how the Milky Way formed, evolved, and was enriched over cosmic time.

Galactic Archaeology

I use stars as fossil records of the early Galaxy, tracing globular cluster debris, disk evolution, and the assembly history of the Milky Way.

Data-Driven Spectroscopy

I build machine-learning tools for large stellar surveys including Gaia and GALAH, using spectra to infer abundances, classifications, and rare stellar populations at scale.

Chemically Peculiar Stars

I study unusual stars, including globular cluster-origin field stars and europium-enhanced stars, to probe rare enrichment pathways and hidden channels of Galactic evolution.

Advocacy

Disability & Albinism Advocacy

As a legally blind astronomer with albinism, I advocate for more accessible science and for the rights, dignity, and visibility of people with albinism.

  • I work on accessible astronomy, including research on data sonification as a tool for scientific communication and inclusion.
  • I have hosted community-building STEM conversations for people with albinism through the National Organization for Albinism and Hypopigmentation.
  • In 2026, I delivered statements to the United Nations Human Rights Council on employment rights and protections for people with albinism and Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome.
  • I have also received training from the UN Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights on engaging with international human rights mechanisms.

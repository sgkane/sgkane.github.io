Research

My research uses stellar spectroscopy and chemical compositions to understand how the Milky Way evolved and was enriched over cosmic time.

Globular Clusters

Globular clsuters are groups of thousands to millions of densely-packed stars and are hypothesized to be important building blocks for the early Galaxy. I am especially interested in stars with chemistry consistent with having formed in a globular cluster that have "escaped" their birth cluster into the Milky Way field. Such stars are invaluable tracers of disrupted and dissolved star clusters.

Data-Driven Spectroscopy

I build machine learning tools for large stellar surveys including Gaia and GALAH, using spectra to infer abundances, classifications, and rare stellar populations in an era of large astronomical surveys.

Chemically Peculiar Stars

"The stranger, the better!" I am interested in the Galaxy's strangest stars and what they can tell us about unique formation enviroments and enrichment sources. Recently, I have been especially interested in metal-rich europium-enhanced stars and what they can tell us about the rapid neutron capture process.

A value-added catalogue of neural network-based europium abundances for GALAH DR4

Sarah G. Kane*, Zofia Kaczmarek*, Andrew Garner, Sven Buder, and Stephanie Monty *These authors contributed equally.

Abstract

Europium traces rare nucleosynthetic events such as neutron-star mergers, but GALAH DR4 reports less precise europium measurements than earlier releases. We train a convolutional neural network to transfer GALAH DR3 [Eu/H] labels onto DR4 spectra and publish predictions for 118,946 stars, including a high-confidence sample of 54,068 giants. We supplement the catalogue with synthesis-based abundances for metal-poor stars. Combined with GALAH DR3, the results provide more than 100,000 vetted abundances on a consistent scale and recover established trends in Galactic chemical evolution.

2026

On the connection between nitrogen-enhanced field stars and the Galactic globular clusters

Sarah G. Kane, Vasily Belokurov, Stephanie Monty, Holger Baumgardt, Carrie Filion, Andrey Kravtsov, GyuChul Myeong, and HanYuan Zhang

Abstract

Nitrogen-enhanced field stars preserve chemical evidence of globular cluster origins after escaping their birth environments. We compare their integrals of motion and metallicities with those of surviving clusters and chemically typical halo stars. Relative to the full halo, these stars are more strongly associated with initially massive clusters in the inner Galaxy. That excess largely disappears when the comparison sample is matched in orbital energy, suggesting that many low-energy inner-halo stars may share cluster origins. Dynamical friction and the Galactic bar further limit reliable present-day star-to-cluster associations.

2025

The ones that got away: chemical tagging of globular cluster-origin stars with Gaia BP/RP spectra

Sarah G. Kane, Vasily Belokurov, Miles Cranmer, Stephanie Monty, Hanyuan Zhang, and Anke Ardern-Arentsen

Abstract

Stars with unusually high nitrogen and aluminium abundances can trace globular clusters even after those stars enter the Galactic field. We train a neural network on low-resolution Gaia BP/RP spectra to identify this rare chemical signature. The classifier reaches approximately 99 per cent accuracy with a false-positive rate near 7 per cent and identifies 878 new candidates. Validation tests show that candidates occur more frequently in clusters than in the field. Most lie in the inner Galaxy, while their field frequency declines near [Fe/H] = -1, around the completion of Galactic disc spin-up.

2024

A Universe of Sound: Processing NASA Data into Sonifications to Explore Participant Response

Kimberly K. Arcand*, Jessica S. Schonhut-Stasik*, Sarah G. Kane*, Gwynn Sturdevant*, Matt Russo, Megan Watzke, Brian Hsu, and Lisa F. Smith *These authors contributed equally.

Abstract

Astronomy communication has historically prioritized visual presentation, limiting access for blind and low-vision audiences and overlooking other ways of understanding scientific information. We presented sonifications of three NASA astronomical objects to blind, low-vision, and sighted participants, then surveyed responses related to learning, enjoyment, and trust. Across 3,184 participants, both audience groups reported learning gains and positive experiences. The findings indicate that multisensory astronomy can expand access, strengthen engagement with scientific data, and increase awareness of accessibility among both sighted and blind or low-vision communities.

Sonification

Sonification is the representation of data as sound and is directly analogous to data visualization. A famous and long-used example of sonification is the Geiger counter, which clicks to report the amount of ionizing radiation present. I research data sonification as a tool for scientific communication, education, and inclusion. It has long been my belief that sonification is engaging and enjoyable for both sighted and blind/low-vision audiences. Included below are a few articles in the press about sonification.

Albinism Advocacy

Albinism is a rare genetic condition that causes a lack of pigmentation in the eyes and typically the skin and hair. It is usually associated with some degree of visual impairment. Some types of albinism, particularly Hermansky-Pudlak Syndrom, are associated with additional health complications.

Around the world, people with albinism face a range of human rights challenges. I have been to the United Nations in Geneva three times to represent the National Organization of Albinism and Hypopigmentation (NOAH) and the Hermanksy-Pudlak Syndrome (HPS) Network. You can watch my statements to the UN Human Rights Council, made on behalf of NOAH and/or the HPS Network, on the UN web archive. Outside of global advocacy, I am a lifelong member of NOAH and have also enjoyed connecting with the Albinism Fellowship of the UK and Ireland since moving to Cambridge.
